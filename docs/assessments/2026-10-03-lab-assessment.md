## 1. Executive Summary

The lab has changed more in three days than most platforms change in a quarter, and it now behaves like a small production platform:
- **GitOps manages the platform itself:** Argo CD, Traefik, the controllers, the projects, SSO, policy, metrics and logs.
- **A cold rebuild is a single, tested path:** 8 min 15 s from an empty host, with no manual steps.
- **Tenants self-register** through a data file.
- **Prod is gated** behind a full commit SHA and signed images.
- **Each environment has its own cloud account.**
- **Every failure seen so far has an alert** that was proven by causing the failure.

The remaining gaps sit one level up, in **the integrity of the paths that feed the platform**:
- The repository that now holds the prod promotion (`tenant-workloads`) has **no branch protection**.
- The **golden chart** that every environment deploys is re-published under the same version on every push, which bypasses prod's pinned values.
- **Image verification** only applies to images named `ghcr.io/brunobml/orders-processor*`, so any other image is admitted unverified.
- **No GitOps repository has CI or CODEOWNERS.** One malformed tenant file stops reconciliation for all tenants.
- The **docs that new engineers start from**, the README Quick Start and the new drills playbook, describe steps that do not work as written.

**Maturity score: 8.0 / 10** (up from 5.5):
- **Architecture and operations: about 8.5.** Self-managed platform, reproducible rebuild, observability with tested alerts.
- **Security: about 7.5.** Identity, least privilege and runtime admission are strong; supply-chain and change-control integrity of the GitOps sources lag behind.

Most of what remains is a short list of configuration changes (branch protection, a version guard, a policy scope, CI checks) rather than new components.

## 2. Strengths

- **The platform manages itself through GitOps.** Argo CD (manual-sync self-management), hub Traefik, kro, ACK, Kyverno, Keycloak, oauth2-proxy, Prometheus, Grafana, Loki and Alloy are all Argo CD Applications. Zero Helm releases remain on the hub. A spoke opts into addons through cluster labels.
- **The rebuild is proven, fast and fully scripted.** `make teardown → setup → bootstrap → post-bootstrap` rebuilt the whole estate in **8 min 15 s** with no manual change. Secrets are regenerated or reused from `~/.config/gitops-lab` (mode 600) and never come from Git.
- **Least privilege at sync time.** Argo CD impersonates per-project destination ServiceAccounts. `argocd-manager` is read + impersonate only. The tenant project is limited to the golden-path kinds. kro runs with aggregated RBAC. The audit script verifies all of this (`RESULT: PASS`).
- **Identity is real.**
  - Keycloak SSO is used for Argo CD (PKCE), Headlamp (oauth2-proxy) and Grafana (OIDC), with group-based roles.
  - Logging out ends the IdP session everywhere.
  - A local break-glass account works without Keycloak, and the built-in `admin` is disabled.
- **Cloud isolation per environment.** ACK CARM maps nonprod to account 111111111111 and prod to 222222222222. Workers use per-environment IAM users, and prod resources have `deletion-policy: retain`.
- **Prod promotion gate in the template.** A prod registration must pin `valuesRevision` to a full 40-character SHA; the ApplicationSet refuses to render anything else. The blueprints reach prod only through `platform-catalog` tags.
- **A signed, scanned, attested application supply chain.**
  - `orders-processor` CI pins every action by SHA and gates on Trivy.
  - It signs keyless with cosign and attests an SPDX SBOM.
  - Kyverno verifies signature *and* attestation at admission, fails closed for tenant namespaces, and runs 2 replicas with a PDB.
- **Observability with alerts that have been fired on purpose.**
  - Metrics from all three clusters; 17 alert rules, each with a runbook section and 12 `promtool` unit tests.
  - Every live-testable alert was triggered by causing its failure (moto down, broken token, F-1 key loss, Kyverno down, OOM, Loki/Alloy down).
  - A synthetic order probe exercises every tenant environment every 5 minutes.
- **Logs and events outlive pods.** Loki keeps 7 days of pod logs and Kubernetes events from all clusters. The `ContainerOOMKilled` alert links straight to the affected namespace's logs.
- **Self-healing operations.** `make post-bootstrap` renews tokens when fewer than 7 days are left, re-provisions lost worker keys, restarts stale workers one pod at a time, and removes orphans of deregistered apps.
- **Drift heals quickly.** Live test: a DLQ deleted behind ACK's back was recreated in **11 s** (worst case = 300 s resync), with the redrive policy intact. In the first assessment this went undetected for up to 10 hours.
- **Hardened workload namespaces.** Tenant and monitoring namespaces enforce Pod Security `restricted`. Tenant namespaces have default-deny NetworkPolicies, and the `queuebackedservice-contract` ValidatingAdmissionPolicy enforces the blueprint's inputs.
- **An excellent paper trail.** Every change since 2026-09-30 has a plan, a peer sign-off, an implementation report and an independent validation, and the remediation records are now archived per assessment.

## 4. Prioritized Recommendations

### Quick wins (under 1 hour each)

1. **Protect `tenant-workloads/main`** with the same rules as the other four repos, and add `CODEOWNERS` that requires the platform owner on `tenants/*/apps/*-prod.yaml` and on the RGD, policy and AppProject files. *Learning:* the promotion gate is only as strong as the repository that holds it. *Prod:* separation of duties for prod changes. **Effort: S.**
2. **Make chart versions immutable.** In `platform-charts/release.yaml`, skip (or fail) when `oci://…/<chart>:<version>` already exists, and push only charts whose version changed. Bump `queue-backed-service` on any template change, and promote the new version per environment (prod pinned). **Effort: S.**
3. **Close the image-verification bypass.** Add a second Kyverno rule for tenant namespaces that denies images outside an allowlist of registries/repositories (for example only `ghcr.io/brunobml/*`), so "not named like orders-processor" can no longer mean "not checked". Keep the signature rule for the allowed repositories. **Effort: S.**
4. **Fix the README Quick Start** (add `make post-bootstrap` after `make bootstrap`, mention it after `make start`, list Grafana and the SSO users, and correct the repo count). **Effort: S.**
5. **Fix drills 2, 5 and 6.**
   - Drills 2 and 6: use a Pod Security-compliant pod and an image the policy actually covers (an unsigned `orders-processor` digest), so the drill proves Kyverno, not Pod Security.
   - Drill 5: use the real registration format.
   - All drills: replace absolute paths with `$(REPOS_DIR)`-style variables.
   **Effort: S.**
6. **Pin the remaining platform images by digest:** Argo CD, Redis and Headlamp through chart values, and kro and ACK through their values in `platform-catalog`. **Effort: S.**
7. **Clean up host and repo leftovers:** stop or delete the `argolab` k3d cluster and the unused `k3d-registry` if no longer needed, remove `dist/` and the deprecated chart archive from `platform-charts`, and add status banners to the two older design docs. **Effort: S.**

### Medium effort (half a day to a day each)

8. **CI for the GitOps repositories.**
   - `gitops-control-plane`: render every Application/ApplicationSet (`helm template`, `kubectl kustomize`, `argocd appset generate` offline where possible), run `make test-alert-rules`, `kubeconform`, and the secret-pattern scan.
   - `tenant-workloads`: validate every registration against a JSON schema (fields, `env` enum, 40-hex prod SHA, unique `<app>-<env>`).
   - `platform-catalog`: render and validate the blueprints and policies.
   - Make these checks **required** on the protected branches.
   **Effort: M.**
9. **Limit the blast radius of a bad registration.** Besides pre-merge validation, consider one ApplicationSet per tenant (Git-files generator rooted at `tenants/<tenant>/`), so one tenant's broken file cannot freeze the others. Also add an alert on the ApplicationSet `ErrorOccurred` condition (kube-state-metrics custom resource state, or an Argo CD metric). **Effort: M.**
10. **Platform namespace baseline.** Add default-deny plus explicit-allow NetworkPolicies to `traefik`, `headlamp`, `oauth2-proxy`, `kro`, `ack-system`, `kyverno` and the spoke `monitoring` namespace. Label them with the strictest Pod Security level each passes (dry-run first, as in Phase 5 F.0). **Effort: M.**
11. **Tenant resource governance.** Have the blueprint (or the tenant ApplicationSet's namespace metadata plus an addon) create a `ResourceQuota` and `LimitRange` per tenant namespace, sized per environment. Optionally add an HPA to the blueprint. **Effort: M.**
12. **Encryption at rest and audit logging.** Enable k3s `--secrets-encryption` and an API audit policy (metadata level for everything, request level for Secrets and RBAC) on all three clusters through `setup-hub-spoke.sh`. Ship the audit log through Alloy to Loki. *Prod:* EKS KMS envelope encryption + control-plane audit logs. **Effort: M.**
13. **Spoke Traefik under GitOps.** Disable the k3s-bundled Traefik on the spokes (`--disable=traefik@server:*`, as on the hub) and deploy the same pinned Traefik chart through a spoke addon ApplicationSet. **Effort: M.**

### Strategic (multi-day)

14. **Signed and verified platform artefacts.** Sign the golden chart (cosign, keyless in the `platform-charts` workflow, SHA-pinned Actions) and verify it before use. Extend Kyverno verification to platform images (Argo CD, kro, ACK, Kyverno itself) where upstream signatures exist. **Effort: L.**
15. **Automated dependency updates.** Renovate (or Dependabot) across the five repos for chart versions, image digests and Action SHAs, with the CI from recommendation 8 as the gate. This keeps digest pinning sustainable. **Effort: M–L.**
16. **Notifications and scheduled self-healing.** A delivery channel for critical alerts (one receiver; owner decision O-1 left this open). Argo CD notifications for sync failures. A scheduled run of the token-renewal/orphan steps, so nothing depends on remembering `make post-bootstrap`. **Effort: M.**
17. **Production identity and transport.** Before Phase 6 (EKS): Keycloak in production mode with an external database (or the managed IdP), TLS on every UI and on spoke→hub telemetry, and per-signal credentials. **Effort: L.**

## 7. Final Architect's Verdict

The first assessment's verdict was "turn the platform on itself". You did, completely: the hub now owns its own control plane, identity, policy, telemetry and recovery, and you proved it by tearing everything down and rebuilding it from Git in eight minutes. Few labs, and not many production platforms, can show a tested cold-start path, alerts that have each been fired on purpose, and a written record of every change.

What remains is not about the running system but about **the integrity of the inputs that drive it**. Three ways around the gates you built are now the weakest links:
- a mutable chart version that reaches prod;
- an unprotected repository that holds the prod promotion;
- an image policy that only checks images with the right name.

All three are small fixes. Add CI on the GitOps repositories and the platform namespace baseline, and the security posture catches up with the operational one.

Do the seven quick wins first; recommendations 1–3 close the three bypasses in under an hour. Then do 8–10. After that, the next real step is the one already planned: translating this to EKS (Phase 6), where most of the remaining parity rows become managed services rather than lab code.
